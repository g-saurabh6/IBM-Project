# Incident Response Simulation

## Scenario: Phishing Attack

### 1. Incident Description

An employee of the small business receives an email that appears to be from a trusted service. The email contains a suspicious link asking the employee to verify their account.

The employee clicks the link and enters their login credentials. Shortly afterward, unusual login activity is detected on the account.

This scenario demonstrates how the organization's Incident Response Plan can be applied to a real-world cybersecurity incident.

## 2. Incident Timeline

| Time | Event | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 AM | Employee receives suspicious email | Email is reviewed |
| 09:05 AM | Employee clicks the link | Credentials may be compromised |
| 09:15 AM | Unusual login detected | Security team investigates |
| 09:20 AM | Account is identified as compromised | Account is disabled |
| 09:30 AM | Password is reset | MFA is verified |
| 10:00 AM | System investigation completed | No further compromise detected |
| 10:30 AM | Account restored | User can safely resume work |

## 3. Incident Response Process

### Phase 1: Identification

The security administrator detects an unusual login from an unexpected location.

The incident is classified as a potential phishing-related account compromise.

### Phase 2: Containment

The compromised account is temporarily disabled.

The security administrator:

- Blocks suspicious access.
- Terminates active sessions.
- Prevents further unauthorized activity.
- Checks whether other accounts are affected.

### Phase 3: Eradication

The security team removes the cause of the incident.

Actions include:

- Resetting the compromised password.
- Checking the device for malware.
- Reviewing account activity.
- Removing unauthorized access.
- Confirming that MFA is enabled.

### Phase 4: Recovery

The account is restored after security checks are completed.

The employee is instructed to:

- Use a new strong password.
- Enable MFA.
- Avoid suspicious links.
- Report similar emails immediately.

Systems are monitored for additional suspicious activity.

### Phase 5: Lessons Learned

After the incident, the organization reviews the event.

The review identifies that:

- Employees need regular phishing awareness training.
- Suspicious emails should be reported immediately.
- MFA provides an additional layer of protection.
- Login activity should be monitored.
- Password security should be regularly reviewed.

## 4. Incident Severity

**Incident Type:** Phishing / Account Compromise

**Severity:** High

**Potential Impact:**

- Unauthorized account access
- Data exposure
- Financial loss
- Business disruption
- Reputation damage

## 5. Preventive Measures

To reduce the possibility of similar incidents:

- Conduct regular phishing awareness training.
- Use multi-factor authentication.
- Implement email filtering.
- Maintain strong password policies.
- Monitor unusual login activity.
- Keep systems and security software updated.

## 6. Final Outcome

The compromised account was successfully contained and recovered.

No major business data loss was identified during the simulation.

The organization improved its security awareness and updated its preventive measures based on the lessons learned.

## 7. Conclusion

This simulation demonstrates how a small business can use a structured Incident Response Plan to identify, contain, eradicate, and recover from a phishing-related security incident.

Regular testing and employee awareness can significantly improve the organization's ability to respond to real cybersecurity incidents.
